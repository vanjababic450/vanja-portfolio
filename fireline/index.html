LONG STORY SHORT

I designed a wildfire safety app grounded in a real 2026 wildfire in Serbia.

In August 2026, a wildfire burned for weeks through Deliblatska Peščara, a nature reserve near Belgrade. Villages were evacuated. International crews flew in to help. Residents pieced together what was happening from broadcasts and word of mouth.

How do we support urgent action when action is required, while keeping the experience calm and informative when it isn’t?

PRIMARY USER

A resident living in or near a threatened settlement

COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

Fire data is abundant. Resident context is not.

Fire data already exists through regional and European tools, and Serbia already has an official alert channel. The opportunity for Fireline is the space between them: ongoing, location-specific context after the initial alert.

DESIGNING THE EXPERIENCE

Designing for changing threat levels

The interface changes with the situation, keeping monitoring calm and making official action impossible to miss.

01 — MONITORING

Nearby fire, no action required

Keep the experience calm: show local status, nearby activity, the latest confirmed update, and whether official action applies.

ADD MONITORING SCREEN
02 — CONDITIONS CHANGING

New activity is moving closer

Surface what changed and what is still developing without implying an official action that has not been issued.

ADD CHANGING CONDITIONS SCREEN
03 — OFFICIAL ACTION

Official guidance takes over

When action is required, prioritize the instruction, source, time, destination, and route when those details are officially provided.
